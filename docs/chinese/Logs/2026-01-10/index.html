Asking for Directions or Locations​

In Chinese, basic direction words are single characters. These can be used alone or combined with 面 (miàn) meaning "side" or "surface".

哪
nǎ
where
那
nà
that
那儿
nàr
there
上
shàng
up
下
xià
down
左
zuǒ
left
右
yòu
right
面
miàn
side

Adding 面 (miàn)​

The suffix 面 is optional and adds the meaning of "side" or "direction":

  • 左 (zuǒ) = left → 左面 (zuǒmiàn) = left side
  • 右 (yòu) = right → 右面 (yòumiàn) = right side

Phrases Using Where / There / That​

你在哪里？
nǐ zài nǎlǐ?
Where are you?
哪里 (nǎlǐ) and 哪儿 (nǎr) both mean "where"
我在这里。
wǒ zài zhèlǐ.
I am here.
那是什么？
nà shì shénme?
What is that?
书在那儿。
shū zài nàr.
The book is there.
厕所在哪儿？
cèsuǒ zài nǎr?
Where is the bathroom?
哪里 (nǎlǐ) and 哪儿 (nǎr) both mean "where"
在那面。
zài nàmiàn.
Over there.

Example Dialogue​

💬Asking for Directions
T
你好，银行在哪里？
nǐ hǎo, yínháng zài nǎlǐ?
Hello, where is the bank?
L
银行在左面。
yínháng zài zuǒmiàn.
The bank is on the left.
T
商店呢？
shāngdiàn ne?
What about the store?
呢 (ne) is used to ask "what about...?"
L
商店在右面。
shāngdiàn zài yòumiàn.
The store is on the right.
T
谢谢！
xièxiè!
Thank you!
