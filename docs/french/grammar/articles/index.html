📚 Articles

Articles are small words that accompany nouns to indicate their definiteness, indefiniteness, or partitivity. In French, articles must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify.

Definition & Overview​

An article (un article) is a word placed before a noun to indicate whether the noun is specific or unspecific, countable or uncountable. French articles reflect the gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) of the noun.

Definite Articles​

Definite articles refer to specific nouns. In French, they are le, la, les, and l'.

Gender/NumberArticleExampleEnglish Translation
Masculine Singularlele livrethe book
Feminine Singularlala maisonthe house
Plurallesles enfantsthe children
Singular (Starting with Vowel)l'l'amithe friend
Singular (Starting with H)l'l'histoirethe story
  • l' is used before singular nouns (masculine or feminine) that begin with a vowel or a silent 'h'.

Indefinite Articles​

Indefinite articles refer to non-specific nouns. In French, they are un, une, and des.

Gender/NumberArticleExampleEnglish Translation
Masculine Singularunun chiena dog
Feminine Singularuneune fleura flower
Pluraldesdes livressome books

Partitive Articles​

Partitive articles refer to an unspecified quantity, often of something uncountable (like food or drink). In French, they are du, de la, de l', and des.

Gender/NumberArticleExampleEnglish Translation
Masculine Singulardudu painsome bread
Feminine Singularde lade la soupesome soup
Pluraldesdes fruitssome fruits
Singular (Starting with Vowel)de l'de l'eausome water
Singular (Starting with H)de l'de l'argentsome money
  • de l' is used before singular nouns (masculine or feminine) that begin with a vowel or a silent 'h'.

Special Cases​

  1. Negative Sentences: In negative sentences, indefinite and partitive articles become de or d'.

    • Example: Je veux du pain → Je ne veux pas de pain. (I want some bread → I don’t want any bread)

  2. Expressions of Quantity: After expressions of quantity, use de without an article.

    • Example: beaucoup de livres (a lot of books), un peu de sucre (a little sugar)

  3. With Certain Verbs: Some verbs, like aimer, adorer, détester, and préférer, always use the definite article.

    • Example: J'aime le chocolat. (I love chocolate)

Usage Notes & Pitfalls​

  • Articles must always agree in gender and number with the noun.
  • Omission of the article is rare in French; most nouns require one.
  • After "ne...pas", "ne...jamais", etc., use de instead of the partitive or indefinite article.

Example Sentences​

  • Le chat est sur la table.
    (The cat is on the table.)
  • J’ai une idée.
    (I have an idea.)
  • Nous mangeons du pain.
    (We are eating some bread.)
  • Elle n’a pas de frères.
    (She doesn’t have any brothers.)
  • Il boit de l’eau.
    (He drinks water.)

Practice & Exercises​

  1. Choose the correct article:

    • ___ pomme (apple)
    • ___ enfants (children)
    • ___ eau (water)
    • ___ livre (book)

  2. Rewrite in the negative:

    • J’ai un chien.
    • Nous mangeons du fromage.
    • Elle a des amis.

  3. Translate to French:

    • The house
    • Some bread
    • A flower
    • The friends
