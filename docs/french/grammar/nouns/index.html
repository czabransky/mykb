📚 Nouns

Nouns are words that name people, places, things, or ideas. In French, nouns have a gender (masculine or feminine) and a number (singular or plural). Understanding these features is essential for correct grammar and agreement.

Definition & Overview​

A noun (un nom) is a word that identifies a person, place, thing, or idea. In French, every noun has a gender ( masculine or feminine) and a number (singular or plural). This affects the articles and adjectives that accompany the noun.

Gender of Nouns​

Every French noun is either masculine or feminine. The gender affects articles, adjectives, and pronouns used with the noun.

How to Identify Gender​

Ending/TypeUsually MasculineUsually Feminine
-age, -ment, -eaule fromage, le gouvernement, le bureau
-isme, -oir, -smele tourisme, le miroir
-é, -teurle café, le chanteur
-e, -ionla pomme, la nation
-té, -ée, -iela liberté, la journée
-ure, -ade, -ancela voiture, la salade, la chance

Note: There are exceptions! Always check a dictionary if unsure.

Pluralization of Nouns​

Most French nouns form the plural by adding -s to the singular form.

SingularPluralExample SentenceEnglish Translation
le livreles livresJ'ai acheté deux livres.I bought two books.
la maisonles maisonsLes maisons sont anciennes.The houses are old.

Irregular Plurals​

Some nouns have irregular plural forms:

SingularPluralExample SentenceEnglish Translation
le chevalles chevauxLes chevaux courent dans le champ.The horses run in the field.
l’animalles animauxLes animaux dorment la nuit.The animals sleep at night.
le travailles travauxLes travaux commencent demain matin.The work starts tomorrow morning.

Nouns ending in -s, -x, or -z do not change in the plural:

  • le prix → les prix
  • le nez → les nez

Common Patterns & Exceptions​

  • Nouns borrowed from other languages are usually masculine.
  • Some nouns can be both masculine and feminine, with a change in meaning (e.g., le livre = book, la livre = pound).
  • Some professions and roles have both masculine and feminine forms: un acteur / une actrice.
  • Some nouns are always singular or plural (e.g., les vacances is always plural).

Usage Notes & Pitfalls​

  • Articles and adjectives must agree in gender and number with the noun.
  • Gender is not always predictable; memorize with the article (e.g., la table, le problème).
  • Some nouns change meaning depending on gender (e.g., le tour = trip, la tour = tower).
  • When in doubt, consult a dictionary for the correct gender.

Example Sentences​

  • Le chat dort sur le canapé.
    (The cat sleeps on the sofa.)
  • La voiture rouge est rapide.
    (The red car is fast.)
  • Les enfants jouent dans le jardin.
    (The children play in the garden.)
  • J’ai acheté des pommes au marché.
    (I bought apples at the market.)
  • Mon ami est médecin.
    (My friend is a doctor.)
