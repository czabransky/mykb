# 📚 Articles

Articles are small words that accompany nouns to indicate their definiteness, indefiniteness, or partitivity. In French,
articles must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify.

## Definition & Overview

An **article** (*un article*) is a word placed before a noun to indicate whether the noun is specific or unspecific,
countable or uncountable. French articles reflect the gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) of the
noun.

---

## Definite Articles

Definite articles refer to specific nouns. In French, they are **le**, **la**, **les**, and **l'**.

| Gender/Number                  | Article | Example         | English Translation |
|:-------------------------------|:--------|:----------------|---------------------|
| Masculine Singular             | le      | **le** livre    | the book            |
| Feminine Singular              | la      | **la** maison   | the house           |
| Plural                         | les     | **les** enfants | the children        |
| Singular (Starting with Vowel) | l'      | **l'ami**       | the friend          |
| Singular (Starting with H)     | l'      | **l'histoire**  | the story           |

- **l'** is used before singular nouns (masculine or feminine) that begin with a vowel or a silent 'h'.

---

## Indefinite Articles

Indefinite articles refer to non-specific nouns. In French, they are **un**, **une**, and **des**.

| Gender/Number      | Article | Example        | English Translation |
|:-------------------|:--------|:---------------|---------------------|
| Masculine Singular | un      | **un** chien   | a dog               |
| Feminine Singular  | une     | **une** fleur  | a flower            |
| Plural             | des     | **des** livres | some books          |

---

## Partitive Articles

Partitive articles refer to an unspecified quantity, often of something uncountable (like food or drink). In French,
they are **du**, **de la**, **de l'**, and **des**.

| Gender/Number                  | Article | Example         | English Translation |
|:-------------------------------|:--------|:----------------|---------------------|
| Masculine Singular             | du      | **du** pain     | some bread          |
| Feminine Singular              | de la   | **de la** soupe | some soup           |
| Plural                         | des     | **des** fruits  | some fruits         |
| Singular (Starting with Vowel) | de l'   | **de l'eau**    | some water          |
| Singular (Starting with H)     | de l'   | **de l'argent** | some money          |

- **de l'** is used before singular nouns (masculine or feminine) that begin with a vowel or a silent 'h'.

---

## Special Cases

1. **Negative Sentences**: In negative sentences, indefinite and partitive articles become **de** or **d'**.
    - Example: Je veux **du** pain → Je ne veux pas **de** pain. (I want some bread → I don’t want any bread)

2. **Expressions of Quantity**: After expressions of quantity, use **de** without an article.
    - Example: beaucoup **de** livres (a lot of books), un peu **de** sucre (a little sugar)

3. **With Certain Verbs**: Some verbs, like **aimer**, **adorer**, **détester**, and **préférer**, always use the
   definite article.
    - Example: J'aime **le** chocolat. (I love chocolate)

---

## Usage Notes & Pitfalls

- Articles must always agree in gender and number with the noun.
- Omission of the article is rare in French; most nouns require one.
- After "ne...pas", "ne...jamais", etc., use **de** instead of the partitive or indefinite article.

---

## Example Sentences

- **Le chat est sur la table.**  
  (The cat is on the table.)
- **J’ai une idée.**  
  (I have an idea.)
- **Nous mangeons du pain.**  
  (We are eating some bread.)
- **Elle n’a pas de frères.**  
  (She doesn’t have any brothers.)
- **Il boit de l’eau.**  
  (He drinks water.)
